Human-Centric Energy Workshop
A Creative Journey Toward Understanding Sustainability
The Human-Centric Energy Workshop was designed within the LAUDSEXP-01-43 project to transform the technical idea of energy management into a hands-on, human experience. It invites participants to discover how creativity, empathy, and technology can merge to shape a more sustainable future — from imagination to prototyping.

Rather than teaching energy efficiency as a theory, the workshop turns it into a collective story. Through creative drama, guided discussions, 3D printing, and artistic co-creation, participants explore the emotional and practical sides of sustainability — and see their own role within it.
Concept and Aims
The workshop was built on three guiding principles:
1. Human connection before information – people learn better when they feel relaxed and connected.
2. Active participation over passive listening – real understanding emerges through doing.
3. Integration of art, technology, and awareness – sustainability becomes tangible when it is seen, felt, and created together.

It is a modular format adaptable to different age groups, spaces, and durations. Each session contributes to a gradual transformation — from self-awareness to collective creation.
1. Ice-Breaking and Trust Building (Creative Drama Session)
Purpose:
To help participants get to know each other, build confidence, and open up communication.

How it works:
Facilitators start with playful drama games like 'Who Are You?', 'Do What I Say', or 'Say What I Say but Not What I Do'. These simple, fun tasks reduce tension and help the group become more responsive and cooperative.

Why it matters:
Energy and sustainability discussions require openness. This warm-up phase prepares participants to think creatively and engage without fear of being wrong.
2. Question Cards & Guided Discussion
Purpose:
To awaken imagination and connect personal ideas to sustainability concepts.

How it works:
Each participant draws or selects a question card such as:
- If you were a plant, what type of plant would you be?
- If you were a hero, what would you do to save the Earth?
- If you were an innovator, what would you design with a 3D printer?

Facilitators listen, then weave these answers into a guided conversation. They draw connections between participants’ examples and the broader sustainability pillars — environmental, economic, social, and governance (ESG).

Facilitator’s role:
Not to lecture, but to guide through curiosity. By echoing participants’ ideas and connecting them to real sustainability scenarios, the facilitator gradually shifts the discussion from imagination toward tangible examples — resource use, renewable materials, circular economy, and efficient production methods.

Supporting videos or visuals can be shown here to illustrate examples (QR codes provided in workshop materials).
3. Group Discussion & Prototyping Session
Purpose:
To translate sustainability ideas into hands-on experience.

How it works:
Participants, with expert support, discuss how their creative ideas could become prototypes using 3D printing. They explore how design affects energy and material use, how the Human-Centric EnMS monitors this process, and how each print’s data connects to a Digital Product Passport (DPP) card that displays energy and material consumption in real time.

Tip for replication:
Depending on time and available equipment, this session can be split into two parts — one focused on discussion and one on hands-on printing and monitoring.

Why it matters:
This step connects imagination to practice. It helps participants realize how design decisions, even at a small scale, affect energy and material use.
4. Hands-On Creation Session
Examples: 'Collaborative Canvas' in Hamburg, 'DIY Bioplastics Lab' at Ars Electronica

Purpose:
To conclude the workshop with a shared creative or scientific act that embodies the group’s reflections.

How it works:
Participants co-create an artefact that expresses their collective understanding of sustainability. In Hamburg, they produced a large collaborative painting visualizing energy and material flows through color and form. In Ars Electronica, they made DIY bioplastic filaments from natural materials like corn or potatoes, symbolizing circular design.

Suggested title for replication: 'Hands-On Reflection: Creating Together for a Sustainable Future'

Facilitators are free to adapt this session — the key is collective creation and reflection through making.
5. Reflection and Evaluation
Purpose:
To let participants synthesize and express what they’ve experienced.

How it works:
This closing session uses simple but profound prompts to gather feedback and emotional resonance:
- Say one word that captures how you feel right now.
- What’s the keyword that stayed with you from this workshop?
- Which word from LAUDS (Local, Accessible, Urban, Digital, Sustainable) best represents today’s experience?

Why it matters:
Evaluation here is not a test — it’s a celebration of learning and awareness. It closes the loop from imagination to understanding.


----

Final Note for Facilitators:
This workshop is not a rigid format — it’s an adaptable framework where facilitation replaces instruction and experience replaces explanation. Whether you lead it in a FabLab, classroom, or community space, the goal remains the same: to let people see, feel, and create sustainability — together.
